Executive Summary — Group-Split Data Leakage Fix

Issue #11  |  XGBoost Binary Classifier  |  Data: 2015–2024  |  April 11, 2026

Background

The classifier predicts pup genotype (HET vs WT) from USV syllable features. Each of the 87 mice contributes 6–337 recordings (avg ~109). The previous baseline split recordings randomly — syllables from the same mouse appeared in both train and test sets, inflating reported accuracy.

Data Leakage Confirmed

In the baseline split, 87 out of 87 mice are shared between train and test. The model memorized mouse-specific patterns rather than learning generalizable ASD features.

What Changed

Added --group-split flag to train_classifier.py. When active, the split ensures no mouse appears in more than one set (train / val / test). Mice are split with stratification on genotype label, then rows are selected by mouse membership.

Baseline Split (random, leaky)

Group Split (no leakage)

Mouse overlap: 0 — fully disjoint

Why Did Precision Drop?

1. The model relied heavily on mother_gen

In the baseline, mother_gen contributed 57% of total feature importance. All HET mice have mother_gen=0, but many WT mice also have mother_gen=0. With leakage, the model could distinguish them by memorizing individual mice. Without leakage, it sees mother_gen=0 and over-predicts HET — producing false positives that drop precision from 0.73 to 0.53.

2. Imbalanced test set

The group split produced a test set with 30.7% HET / 69.3% WT (7 vs 11 mice). With fewer HET samples and more WT samples, any false positive weighs heavier against HET precision.

3. Strain distribution mismatch

Train has 63% Strain 1 but test has only 32% Strain 1. The model trains on one distribution and is tested on another, reducing generalization.

AUC-ROC vs Accuracy Paradox

The AUC-ROC on validation reaches ~0.97, yet test accuracy is only 72.1%. This means the model ranks mice correctly (HET gets higher probability than WT), but the decision threshold (0.5) is not calibrated. The model "knows" who is HET, but the cutoff is wrong.

The Classification Error plot also reveals overfitting: after ~10 estimators, validation error rises while train error continues to fall. The current model uses 50 estimators — far past the optimal point.

Feature Importance

mother_gen remains the dominant feature at 37% importance (down from 57% in baseline). Acoustic syllable features gained relative weight, which is a positive sign — the model is starting to use actual vocalization patterns.

Key Takeaway

The baseline accuracy of 82.9% was inflated by ~10 percentage points due to data leakage. The real generalization performance is 72.1%. This is now the honest baseline for future improvements — any gain from here is genuine.

Recommendations for Next Steps

  • Threshold tuning — AUC is 0.97, meaning the model ranks well. Calibrating the decision threshold (instead of default 0.5) could significantly improve precision without retraining.
  • Reduce estimators / early stopping — The error curves show overfitting after ~10 estimators. Reducing n_estimators to 10–15 or adding early stopping on validation error may improve generalization.
  • Remove or reduce mother_gen — It contributes 37% of importance but leaks genetic information that the model over-relies on. Training without it would force the model to learn from acoustic features.
  • Balance strain in split — Train has 63% Strain 1 while test has 32%. A strain-balanced group split could reduce distribution mismatch.
  • Group by mother (not just pup) — Siblings share genetics. Grouping by mother identity would prevent sibling leakage. Requires adding mother ID to the feature pipeline.
