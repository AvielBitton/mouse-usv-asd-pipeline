# 6. Discussion

## 6.1 What works, and in which regime

The results give a clear, calibrated answer to RQ1 and RQ3. Among the new models, **TabPFN is the best generalizer on the pooled cohort**, reaching 0.729 accuracy / 0.743 weighted F1 / 0.783 ROC-AUC on entirely unseen mice — a genuine improvement over the inherited XGBoost (0.693 / 0.706 / 0.770) and, notably, an honest independent score that matches the inherited model's *optimistic* dependent accuracy (0.733). The tuned XGBoost is the best *minority-aware* generalizer, with the highest independent balanced accuracy (0.725) and HT recall (0.869). The choice between them is a deployment decision: if the goal is overall correctness, TabPFN; if the goal is catching as many ASD-model pups as possible (high HT recall) at the cost of more false alarms, the tuned XGBoost or a target-recall threshold (Section 5.5).

The single most decision-relevant finding for the field is the **size of the dependent→independent gap**. Optimistic subject-dependent evaluation overstates generalization by roughly 4–10 accuracy points and, more dramatically, by up to 0.30 in HT recall for the inherited model. Any USV-classification result reported without subject-grouped splits should be read with this in mind. This is the practical payoff of the evaluation protocol that this project introduced.

## 6.2 Why minority precision is capped

The HT-precision wall near 0.50 (Sections 5.3, 5.5) is the most informative negative result. It is invariant to model family (trees vs foundation model), to tuning, and to split regime, and it survives threshold relocation (which trades recall for precision along a fixed ROC curve but cannot raise AUC). The natural conclusion is that the ceiling is set by the **representation, not the estimator**: the per-recording aggregates (mean start/end frequency, relative frequency, and mean duration per syllable type, plus a few metadata fields) summarise each recording into 46 numbers that rank WT and HT well (AUC up to 0.78 on unseen mice) but do not separate the two cleanly at the individual level. The feature-importance analysis (Section 5.7) points in the same direction: the model leans on boundary frequencies and durations — exactly the features Shekel et al. [17] flagged — but lacks richer per-call descriptors such as full frequency contours, bandwidth, and frequency-modulation depth. Enriching the per-call feature set is therefore the most promising route to breaking the wall, more so than larger models.

## 6.3 Sequence order did not help — at this scale

RQ3 asked whether temporal call order adds signal. The sequence models, motivated by Gal et al.'s [18] finding that call dynamics carry phenotypic information, did **not** beat the tabular aggregates, and several collapsed to degenerate predictors despite informative AUCs. Cross-validation deflated the most promising configuration from a single-split 0.704 to 0.563 ± 0.063 balanced accuracy. The most likely explanation is **data scale**: the session-level representation reduces the corpus to 408 sessions with ~19 HT sessions in an independent test fold, far too few for ~70k–150k-parameter networks to learn temporal structure reliably. This is a statement about the present dataset, not about the hypothesis: Gal et al.'s dynamics may well be real and learnable with more animals or with per-mouse aggregation of session predictions. We therefore frame the sequence result as "not supported at this data scale" rather than "temporal order is uninformative".

## 6.4 The strain confound

The per-strain analysis (Section 5.6) is a caution against over-claiming. Strain1 is almost perfectly separable leak-free (0.903), while strain2 collapses on the minority class (HT recall 0.089). Because strain/background, recording year, and HT prevalence all covary with genotype, a model trained within or across strains may be exploiting cohort membership rather than the ASD phenotype. The pooled independent numbers (Section 5.1) are the most defensible single summary precisely because they average across this confound, but even they cannot fully disentangle phenotype from cohort. The honest test — train on one strain, evaluate on the other — is identified as future work (Section 9).

## 6.5 The contribution in perspective

Read together, the scientific results are modest but honest: USVs support **above-chance, AUC-0.78 generalization** to unseen *Mthfr* mice, driven by spectral and temporal syllable features, with a clear feature-level ceiling on precision and no benefit from sequence modelling at this scale. The larger contribution of this iteration is methodological and infrastructural: a reproducible pipeline, a single source of truth, the discovery and correction of a data error that had inflated a prior result, and an evaluation protocol that separates optimistic from honest performance. In a field where descriptive, leakage-prone analyses are common, establishing *what can and cannot be honestly claimed* — and making it reproducible — is itself a result.
